**UML (Unified Modelling Language)** : a **standard** (OMG), **mostly graphical** (in fact *hybrid*), **general-purpose** set of notations for modelling both **problems** (application domain) and **solutions** (software).

- widely used standard for software and systems engineering ; current spec : **UML 2.5.1** (OMG, Dec. 2017)
- maintained by the **Object Management Group (OMG)**, built by adopting common usages
- goal : become the *lingua franca* of software professionals
- semi-formal : well-defined syntax, partial semantics (see [[Modelling Languages]])

## A language, not a method
**Method = notation(s) + process + tools.** UML is only the notation, purely at the language level :
- no development method or process
- no task ordering
- no responsibility definition

## Objectives
1. represent entire systems, from low- to high-level concerns
2. let the modeller choose the **granularity** of the description
3. bind concepts explicitly to **executable artefacts**

Applicable to many domains : business, finance, HR, automotive, avionics, medical, scientific, multimedia…

## History
Unifies the 1990s object-oriented methods : Booch, **OMT** (Rumbaugh) and OOSE (Jacobson) ; standardised by the OMG in 1997, UML 2 in 2005 (general knowledge, the course slide is a timeline image).
